FROM THE IMPERIAL CHRONICLE

Mahendrapal

For generations an emperor almost lost to the historical record, until an extraordinary charter changed the succession.

Mahendrapal, a son of Devpal, was established as a distinct Pal sovereign through the discovery of the Jagjivanpur copperplate in present-day West Bengal.

The inscription illuminates royal patronage and land grants, and demonstrates why earlier published king-lists were incomplete. It is an unusually vivid reminder that medieval history continues to change with archaeological discovery.

The length and exact years of his rule remain disputed, and a cautious account distinguishes him from kings of the same name belonging to other dynasties.
