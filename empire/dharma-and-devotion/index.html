Dharma & Devotion

The sacred art of the Pal age offers a splendid testimony to Hindu worship in Bengal and Bihar. Images of Shiva, Uma, Vishnu, Lakshmi, Sarasvati and other deities were created in black stone and bronze with exceptional refinement.

The sacred art of the Pal age offers a splendid testimony to Hindu worship in Bengal and Bihar. Images of Shiva, Uma, Vishnu, Lakshmi, Sarasvati and other deities were created in black stone and bronze with exceptional refinement.

Narayanpal’s Bhagalpur copperplate documents the support of a Shiva temple, and the Badal inscription preserves the ritual identity of learned Brahmanical ministers. Such evidence makes Hindu practice and patronage inseparable from the history of the period.

The royal house is also historically celebrated for substantial patronage of Buddhist monastic institutions and scholarship. Many dynastic formulas identify its rulers with that tradition. A truthful account must acknowledge these facts while allowing the Hindu artistic and religious inheritance its full presence.

Religious patronage in this period did not always align neatly with modern categories of exclusive identity. A ruler might support institutions of different traditions, and artists worked in shared local workshops and visual idioms.
